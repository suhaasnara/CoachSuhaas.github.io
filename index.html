My Chess Journey

My steps and missteps in reaching 2200 USCF — a lifetime goal.

Chapter 1

Chess When I Was a Kid

I started playing chess at age 4 — nearly two decades of experience across different life stages began with simple curiosity about the pieces. What started as a childhood game quickly revealed itself to be something much deeper.

Early on, chess was about fun. Learning how the knight jumps, why the bishop is sneaky, how a pawn can become a queen. The board felt like a puzzle that never ran out of pieces.

Age 4 — The Beginning

First Moves

Introduced to chess at a young age. Captivated immediately by the infinite complexity on the board.

Early Childhood

Hobby & Passion

Chess fluctuated between hobby and obsession. I got out of it fun and beauty — and wanted more of both.

Chapter 2

Getting Serious

At some point the casual enjoyment wasn't enough. I wanted to compete. My first rated tournaments were humbling — losses taught me that chess knowledge and chess skill are very different things.

I am, by nature, a calm and positional player. Heavily booked up on openings. I play a mix of bullet and rapid online and OTB classical. This shaped the kind of study I gravitated toward.

Tournament Play

Entering Rated Chess

First rated tournaments revealed how much I didn't know. How to handle pressure, recover from blunders, find calm in complex positions. Wins and losses both shaped my understanding.

Deep Study Phase

Immersing in the Game

Openings (perhaps too much of them — fun but not always the best ROI). Tactics (never enough — chess is a lot, maybe not 99%, of tactics). Game review became my favorite training method.

Chapter 3

The Final Push to NM

Reaching 2200 USCF was the culmination of years of deliberate effort. The final push wasn't about suddenly discovering a magic training method — it was about consistency, honest self-assessment, and keeping chess fun enough to keep going.

I'm grateful for every training partner, opponent, coach, and friend who made it worth it. NM really is the friends we made along the way.

The Title

Achieving 2200 USCF

Earning the National Master title validated not just the results but the process. Mastery is built move by move, game by game. There are no shortcuts to getting points — Haikyu.

Today

Giving Back Through Coaching & This Site

Now I channel what I've learned into coaching and into this guide. Watching a student have a breakthrough is as rewarding as any game I've played.

Chapter 4

My Training Routine — Warts and All

This isn't what I recommend. This is an honest look at the path I actually took, so I can make better recommendations to you.

📚

Openings (Too Much)

Fun, but not always great ROI. I over-invested here early on. Openings do matter, but they matter less than most beginners think.

⚡

Tactics (Not Enough)

Chess is a lot of calculation. I didn't study nearly enough positions, not necessarily tactics, especially in my earlier years.

🎮

Training Games & Review

My favorite training method and the one I recommend most. Play serious games and actually review them — without an engine first.

Learning Philosophy

How I think about chess improvement — and what I've learned teaching others.

The Foundation

Core Principles

♟

Understand, Don't Memorize

Improvement comes from grasping the why behind each move. I teach principles so you can reason through positions you've never seen before.

♜

Learn from Every Game

Your losses are your greatest teachers. Analyze your own games without an engine first — this builds the critical thinking that creates improvement.

♞

Endgames First

Most players neglect endgames. I prioritize them because they reveal the true nature of chess: piece activity, king power, and precision. These are fundamentals that flow into every phase.

♛

Consistent, Deliberate Practice

An hour of focused study beats five hours of casual play. I help students build routines that make improvement sustainable over the long run.

🎯

Tactics, Tactics, Tactics

Chess is calculation. No matter your level, a daily tactics habit is the highest-ROI training activity there is.

😄

Do What Makes Chess Fun

This is the one takeaway I want for everyone. A rating goal is not an end in itself. You won't hit your goal if you hate the process — you'll just quit.

"Improving at chess is about work ethic, inspiration, and optimism. Your approach reflects who you are and the values you live by." — Suhaas Narayanan, NM
Know Thyself

Your Chess Player Profile

Some say the whole of life is experiencing yourself. It's useful to understand who you are and what you want to get out of chess. Chess is a marathon. There may be people in their final sprint, but it's a long and hard path even then.

Before diving into a training plan, consider these questions honestly:

🤔

Who Are You?

Your occupation, time commitment, life stage, and outside priorities all shape how much you can realistically invest in chess improvement.

🎯

What Is Chess to You?

Hobby? Obsession? Time-killer? What do you currently get out of chess — and what do you want to get out of it? Be honest.

♟

What Type of Player Are You?

Sharp and attacking, or calm and positional? Booked up or intuitive? Lots of online blitz, or OTB classical only? Know thyself.

⚡

What Drives You?

Identify what keeps you motivated, what causes burnout, and where chess sits in the hierarchy of your life. This shapes everything.

My Example: I'm a calm, positional player who is heavily booked up on openings. I play a mix of bullet and OTB classical. Chess fluctuates between hobby and obsession for me. What I get out of chess is fun, truth, and beauty.
